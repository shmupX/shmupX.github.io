Dezaemon SFC Parity Mapデザエモン

What @shmupx/shmup-engine/sfc decodes of a 1994 Super Famicom Dezaemon battery-SRAM save, and what stands between it and the parity the Saturn map reports. This cart was ported to the PlayStation as Dezaemon+, whose save keeps these region names in a new order — the PSX map. The series then went 3D on the Nintendo 64, whose 64DD data disk has a map of its own. Target is the original cart (Athena, SHVC-66): a little-endian 65C816, 512 KB of LoROM and 128 KB of battery SRAM dumped raw — no header, no interleave, no compression, nothing like the Saturn's BUP image. The ROM documents its own SRAM map, a 26-row ADDRESS NAME debug table at ROM 0x66A5, so every byte is labelled and the parser reads all 26 regions; but the only fixture is the factory sample with its graphics bank all zero, nothing has been traced through an emulator, and as of 2026-09-07 the parser is a read-only structural pass: nothing is played, mapped into game.json or written back. Canonical notes: packages/shmup-engine/FORMAT-SFC.md.

01What remains

The SFC parser landed in one pass (2026-09-07) with the factory sample as its only fixture; on 2026-10-14 the ROM was traced statically — the chip banking, the map rows, the entry fields, the appearance tables, the scroll sequencer and the boot path — against that sample and ALDI Adventure, and a writer landed on it. SOLVED means the layout is traced, documented in FORMAT-SFC.md and consumed by code; MEANING OPEN means the bytes are located but a field's semantics are untraced; NO TRACE means nothing exists yet. Each item ends with what would close it.

  1. 1

    The CHECK SUM algorithmSOLVED

    32 bytes at 0x0000, repeated byte for byte at 0x7E5A, sixteen little-endian words. Traced statically — $00:847B computes, $00:8482 verifies, sharing a body at $00:848A. Each word is 0xFFFF minus the mod-216 sum of the words over a scatter/gather segment list; $80:8665 holds seventeen descriptors. Word 0 covers GRAPIC DATA and PALETTE DATA, 1–6 and 7–C the six stages twice over, D the ship/title/config group, E sound, F the block's own first fifteen words, so the block totals 0xFFFF by construction. SFC_CHECKSUM_DESCRIPTORS is the table transcribed; sealSfcChecksums() writes a save, verifySfcChecksums() checks one, and the probe report says "all 17 words verify" for both dumps. checksum.js · FORMAT-SFC.md

    Also traced: the boot path. Word F (or the copy's word, with the block restored from the copy) gates the load, T.TABATA with four of eight bytes matching keeps the save, and words 0–E only raise a "CHECK SUM" message. RESERVED is never read.

  2. 2

    GRAPIC DATA banking, and where the sample's art livesSOLVED

    A group word is not a VRAM tile number: the chip assembler $80:AC5D reads bits 8–10 as a sheet (0/1 the save's own 32 KB halves, 2/3 the ROM's factory art at 0x40000, 4 the editor's icons), bits 3–7 a chip row and 0–2 a column — offset = base + row × 0x400 + col × 0x40, the 2×2 chip's tiles at +0, +0x20, +0x200, +0x220 — and copies the chip into the SLOT the word occupies; the slot decides the VRAM tile ((i >> 3) × 32 + (i & 7) × 2). Bits 14–15 are the flips, 0xFFFF the empty word drawn as the ROM's blank chip 0x03FF. That is why the sample names only 0x200–0x3FF (its art is the ROM's, read at play time — the data-source flag $19CE) and ALDI only 0x000–0x1FF. Both saves render end to end under it: the stage maps, the ship sheet, the enemy sheets, the bosses. chips.js · groups.js · cover.js

    What closed it: static disassembly of the assembler and the stage assembly's four passes (MAP GROUP → BG tiles 0x200+, MY SHIP → OBJ names 0x000–0xFF, BOSS → BG tiles 0x20–0x9F, ENEMY → OBJ names 0x100–0x1FF), then rendering both dumps. The shelf cover now draws through it, with the ROM when given.

  3. 3

    SOUND DATAMEANING OPEN

    0x5058-0x7E58: 16 songs of 0x2E0 bytes (the editor's clear copies one ROM block, $80:8E46, to all sixteen). BGM PATCH is one song number per slot — 0–5 a stage's song, 6–11 its boss song, 12 the title, 13–15 ending / clear / game over — and values from 0x10 pick the ROM's built-in songs, which is all the sample's patch does: its stream is never played. The bit-packed note format is still unread. The writer carries the sample's 16 songs and the sample's patch, so an exported game sounds like the factory game. tables.js · factory-data.js

    What closes it: the APU upload path ($8F:FC78), or one note added in the composer, saved and diffed; and whether an all-zero song is tolerated.

  4. 4

    ENEMY DATA, APPEAR DATA and the ODR frame listsSOLVED

    ENEMY DATA is 6 stages × 0x200, not 24 × 128: five classes of eight-byte entries — 0, 1, 2 sixteen each (16×16, 32×16, 16×32 sprites), 3 eight (32×32), 4 four (64×64) — then a 32-byte boss block. The object builder $00:A713 reads byte 0 as the movement family (bits 3–7: 0 stationary, 1 straight down, 2–16 authored paths, 17–19 homing and dashes) and variant (speed), byte 1 the shot aim and rate, byte 2 the shot type and the item-drop bit, byte 3 the hit points ({1, 50 … 2000}) and two flags, byte 4 the score ({50 … 10000}) and the animation interval, byte 5 a shot parameter. ENEMY ODR is 320 bytes a stage of slot references into the stage's 64-slot enemy sheet, laid out by class: four 16×16 frames, two frames of two chips, two of four, one of sixteen. APPEAR DATA is 256 records of 14 spawn bytes (one per playfield column 1..14: valid, class, entry) and a 1,024-byte line table — one line per 16 px, one map row — naming the record that fires there; class 5 spawns the boss. enemy.js · groups.js · sfc-write.js

    Still open inside it: byte 1's aim value (the writer copies the sample's), the shot types' shapes, the boss block's pair bytes and score table. What closes them: the shot routine $02:82AD and the phase logic $02:A337, or one enemy under Mesen.

  5. 5

    SCROLL EFECT, MAP DATA bit 7 and the empty and sparse columnsSOLVED

    MAP DATA is 128 rows of 18 bytes: two halves of eight cells and a flag byte — cell bit 7 the horizontal flip, bits 0–6 the MAP GROUP slot, the flag byte's bits the cells' vertical flips. The playfield is 16 chips (256 px) wide; the "18 columns" counted the flag bytes as cells, and the empty columns were those bytes. SCROLL EFECT's first 256 bytes are the map-row-block sequencer ($00:D851): entry i names the 4-row block of the map shown while the scroll runs through its 64 px, so the 128-row map plays in order and repeats across the stage's 1,024 lines by default, or is re-sequenced. The second 256 bytes (0x42) have no reader in the play engine: the scroll advances 1 px a frame, and loops inside the current window while a boss lives. map.js

    What closed it: the stage loader $00:9B9C and the tilemap streamer, then rendering both dumps upright at 16 columns through real chips.

  6. 6

    The game.json mappingNO TRACE

    The read direction still has no SFC map-to-game.js: nothing decoded here reaches the editor's game.json. The pieces now exist — chips decode to pixels, entries to named fields, appearance lines to (column, class, entry) — so a mapper is a matter of assembling them the way the writer disassembles a level. index.js

    What closes it: an SFC map-to-game over the decoded sheets, entries and lines; the writer's report is the inverse's checklist.

  7. 7

    Playing itMEANING OPEN

    The launcher's SNES shelf boots the cart with a save as its .srm (EmulatorJS snes9x), and /api/sfc-save installs a save as Mednafen's SRAM for the ROM on this machine and starts it. What is still untested is a written save under the game: the first built image parses, verifies and renders, and the round trip in Mednafen is the next step.

    What closes it: one run — deno task build:sfc, the editor's → MEDNAFEN (SRAM) row, the cart booting into the game.

  8. 8

    The write pathSOLVED

    exportLevelToSfcSram(level, art) builds the 128 KB image from a level record: the art quantised into the rows the game copies (scenery row n, enemies 6+n, boss 12+n, title 19) and cut into chips of the save's two sheets with mirrors shared; scenery into map rows and group slots; enemies into entries of the class their art fits, their frames into the stage sheet and ENEMY ODR, their waves into appearance records and lines; the boss into the 8×4 sheet with the sample's block and a class-5 spawn; the factory ship sheet and sound; the checksums sealed last; the image read back before it leaves. deno task build:sfc runs it from a shell, the editor's CONSOLE › SFC pick has DOWNLOAD .SRM and → MEDNAFEN (SRAM). sfc-write.js · sfc-format.js · build-sfc.ts

    Templates inside it: the shot aim, the boss block, the ship sheet (the level's own ship is not painted: the pose slots are likely, not traced), and hits × 25 against the hit-point table.

  9. 9

    A second fixtureSOLVED

    ALDI Adventure sits beside the ROM in dev-fixtures/SNES Dezaemon - Kaite Tsukutte Asoberu/, pulled from the SFC library, and the fixture-gated tests read both it and the sample: every claim above was checked against the two, and the checksum table reproduces all seventeen words of each. test/sfc-checksum.test.js · test/sfc-index.test.js

    What would add to it: a save written by a real session, to catch whatever a one-stage test level does not.

02Already decoded

Confirmed The bytes decode as the ROM's label says and a test locks it — never that a runtime consumes them. Rows regions.js calls likely or open say so: the stride is what the code applies, the semantics belong to section 03 — or, for the graphics bank, to item 2.

03Located but not yet understood

Likely What is still read off ranges and samples rather than the engine: a handful of fields inside regions whose layout is now traced.

  • SCROLL EFECT +0x100 0x3A40-… — cleared to 0x42; no reader in the play engine was found, and the scroll advances 1 px a frame regardless
  • ENEMY DATA byte 1 bits 0–4 — the shot aim/pattern parameter the shot routine $02:82AD consumes; the shapes of shot types 0–2
  • The boss block's pair bytes 7–12 and score index — consumed by the phase logic $02:A337; the phases cycle on a timer
  • MY SHIP GROUP slot roles — chip 0 the player, 12 the shot, 16–34 the enemy bullets, 46+ the items, 56 the bomb, 72 the explosion are the pointers; the pose-to-chip order inside slots 0–15 is likely, not read
  • Palette rows 18–21's CGRAM slots — the row-to-CGRAM copy for ship, title and items was not located (enemies OBJ palette 4, map BG 7, boss BG 6/5 are)
  • The player's shot damage against the hit-point table; the writer takes a hit as 25
  • TITLE TYPE's motion fields and KEY CONFIG's bits — decoded as far as their readers' shapes

04No trace at all

Absent What has no code behind it yet.

  • The game.json mapping — no SFC map-to-game.js; the parser stays behind the ./sfc subpath and nothing in the editor reads a cart back into a level (item 6)
  • The sound format — SOUND DATA's 16 songs are sliced and carried, never read or synthesised (item 3)
  • A written save under the game — the writer's output parses, verifies and renders; it has not yet booted in Mednafen or on the shelf (item 7)
  • The level's own ship on the cart — the writer keeps the factory ship sheet until the pose slots are traced
