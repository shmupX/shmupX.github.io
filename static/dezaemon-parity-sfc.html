Dezaemon SFC Parity Mapデザエモン

What @shmupx/shmup-engine/sfc decodes of a 1994 Super Famicom Dezaemon battery-SRAM save, and what stands between it and the parity the Saturn map reports. This cart was ported to the PlayStation as Dezaemon+, whose save keeps these region names in a new order — the PSX map. Target is the original cart (Athena, SHVC-66): a little-endian 65C816, 512 KB of LoROM and 128 KB of battery SRAM dumped raw — no header, no interleave, no compression, nothing like the Saturn's BUP image. The ROM documents its own SRAM map, a 26-row ADDRESS NAME debug table at ROM 0x66A5, so every byte is labelled and the parser reads all 26 regions; but the only fixture is the factory sample with its graphics bank all zero, nothing has been traced through an emulator, and as of 2026-09-07 the parser is a read-only structural pass: nothing is played, mapped into game.json or written back. Canonical notes: packages/shmup-engine/FORMAT-SFC.md.

01What remains

The SFC parser landed in one pass (2026-09-07): the container, the ROM's own map, every fixed table, a probe and 31 tests, with the factory sample save as the only fixture; what follows is what that pass could not settle. SPEC WRITTEN means the layout is documented in FORMAT-SFC.md and only a consumer is missing — no SFC item has earned it yet; MEANING OPEN means the bytes are located — at the ROM's stride for the regions regions.js calls likely, by label alone for the ones it calls open — but their semantics are untraced; NO TRACE means nothing exists yet. Each item ends with what would close it — FORMAT-SFC.md's Method: the ROM in Mesen 2 (SNES) or bsnes-plus with the fixture as its .srm.

  1. 1

    The CHECK SUM algorithmMEANING OPEN

    32 bytes at 0x0000, repeated byte for byte at 0x7E5A as CHECK SUM COPY; readChecksumBlocks() compares the two, and that is the only integrity check today. It is no per-block sum: byte sums, word sums, negated and XOR variants over 2/4/8 KB blocks of 32/64/128 KB all miss. A second dump — NovaSquirrel's ALDI Adventure, 61,374 of 131,072 bytes different — settles what one never could, and it falls both ways. Confirmed: the 16 little-endian words sum to 0xFFFF in both saves (2-32 by chance), and the accumulator discards carry — the words total 0x7FFFF and 0x8FFFF as integers, seven overflows and eight, same residue. Refuted: the old "word 0 equals the PALETTE DATA word sum" lead, which fails outright on ALDI (0x42D4 against a complement of 0x7854) — the sample's match was one of ~13 chance hits its own sweep produces. Also refuted: a per-region table (four regions sum to 0x0000 and would collide, yet all 16 words differ), and any contiguous range sum — requiring a range to hit its target in both dumps leaves zero survivors for the LE word sum across all 16 words, and chance-level counts for BE words and bytes. What remains open is a strided, masked or polynomial scan. sram.js · FORMAT-SFC.md

    What closes it: a write breakpoint on $700000-$70001F during an in-editor save lands in the routine, which lives near the S-RAM CHECK! strings at ROM 0x521. Short of the trace, only controlled deltas help, and by a measured amount: a second save editing every region cuts 7,264 candidates per word to 1.1, one editing only MAP DATA leaves 2,409, one that has merely been played leaves 3,751, and a dump that only fills in the graphics bank buys nothing. Single-byte probe dumps pin every range in ~30–60 shared probes and settle additive-vs-polynomial for free. The write path (item 8) waits on this.

  2. 2

    GRAPIC DATA banking, and where the sample's art livesMEANING OPEN

    0x10000-0x20000, the upper two segments, is by size 2,048 SNES 4bpp planar 8×8 tiles of 32 bytes, and the codec is done and locked (tiles.js, section 02). Unknown is which 32 bytes a group word's 10-bit tile number points at: 64 KB is more than VRAM holds at once, so the game must bank it, and the only fixture has the region zeroed, so nothing has ever been drawn through it. The ROM's last 128 KB (0x60000+) renders as editor UI icon tiles, not as the sample's bank, so where the factory game's art comes from when played is open too. graphics.js

    What closes it: a dump with graphics (item 9), then the emulator's DMA log — SRAM to VRAM gives the banking, SRAM to CGRAM says which palette rows are real and which group feeds which layer.

  3. 3

    SOUND DATAMEANING OPEN

    0x5058-0x7E58: 11,776 bytes at 7.45 bits/byte of entropy — a bit-packed stream, not tiles and not a byte-per-step sequencer; sliceSound() hands it out as stored. What the composer holds is known only from outside the stream (16 bars at a 1/16 quantise in 4/4, two voices, changeable instruments — FORMAT-SFC.md), and BGM PATCH is one instrument number per BGM slot; the stream's own layout is not. Against the Saturn side, where sec6 is decoded and synthesised live in WebAudio, this is the widest single gap — this side plays nothing. tables.js · regions.js

    What closes it: reads of the region during the APU upload ($2140-$2143), and one note added in the composer, saved and diffed.

  4. 4

    ENEMY DATA, APPEAR DATA and the ODR frame listsMEANING OPEN

    The strides are the ROM's and the code applies them — ENEMY DATA 24 records × 128 bytes from 0x8000 (the sample fills 23; record 21 is blank), APPEAR DATA 6 stages × 0x1200 from 0x8C00 (every sample stage opens with fourteen 0x00 then fourteen 0xFF), ENEMY ODR 24 × 80 bytes = 20 four-byte quads per enemy, MY SHIP ODR 16 quads (zero in the sample) — but no field inside any of them is traced: no position, timing, HP or fire byte is named, and the ODR quads that repeat merely look like animation frame lists. On the Saturn map the 18-byte record, the 256 behaviour scripts and the death word are done; here a record is 128 opaque bytes. enemy.js · groups.js

    What closes it: controlled deltas — change one thing in the editor, save, sfc:probe diff — pin fields to offsets and confirm the APPEAR record shape; the ROM under a debugger says what the engine does with them.

  5. 5

    SCROLL EFECT, MAP DATA bit 7 and the empty and sparse columnsMEANING OPEN

    SCROLL EFECT (0x3940-0x4540) is split 6 × 0x200 by size, and that split is a placeholder: the sample opens with 0x100 bytes of small integers, then smooth curves of bytes around 0x40 (0x38-0x47) padded with 0x1F / 0x1D recur every 0x200 from +0x100 — a per-stage scroll-speed or offset table by the label; what indexes it is open. In MAP DATA the width is measured (18 columns, section 02) but bit 7 is set on some cells — 0x9C at cell 1 of stage 0 — and uninterpreted: the chip index masks to 0x7F. Columns 0 and 16 hold no non-zero cell in any stage and columns 8 and 17 are sparse — per-row markers rather than scenery, possibly. map.js

    What closes it: reads of SCROLL EFECT while a stage scrolls; the map rendered through real graphics (item 2); one flagged cell toggled in the editor and diffed.

  6. 6

    The game.json mappingNO TRACE

    The Saturn side has map-to-game.js; nothing SFC exists, and parseSfcSav() is kept behind the ./sfc subpath on purpose — mod.js is the Saturn editor bundle. Nothing decoded here — not the 24 palette rows, not the six 18 × 128 stage maps, not a GROUP table — reaches the editor's game.json or anything the Phaser runtime reads. Even a scenery-only import waits on item 2: a chip's quad is four VRAM tile numbers with no SRAM bytes behind them, and an enemy would arrive as 128 unnamed bytes (item 4). Already on the shelf is the palette rule: the Saturn writer's snes palette target and snesCgramBytes() speak one 15-colour row per sprite, and cgram.js is that function's reader half. index.js · palette-target.js

    What closes it: items 2 and 4. Until then a mapper could carry only palettes, six chip grids without their art, and the high-score table.

  7. 7

    Playing itNO TRACE

    Nothing here plays a Super Famicom cart: static/emulators.json has no SNES core, so the launcher cannot boot the ROM with a save as its .srm; there is no game.json to hand the Phaser runtime; nothing in this repo runs the 65C816 or its APU. Nothing on this page has been traced through emulation: the decoded rows rest on measurements of bytes and labels read out of the ROM, never on an engine trace — which is exactly what the Saturn map's confirmed rows rest on. That side's exported cart boots and plays in Mednafen; this side has never been booted from this repo at all.

    What closes it: an SNES core in the launcher for the cart itself, or item 6 for the runtime — and the debugging emulator of the Method is where items 1–5 get closed.

  8. 8

    The write pathNO TRACE

    No SFC writer. The container needs no builder — a save is the raw 131,072 bytes, four 32 KB segments in banks $70-$73, T.TABATA at 0x7FF8 — and encodeTile4bpp / encodeTile2bpp and palette-target.js's snesCgramBytes() already produce tiles and a 512-byte CGRAM bank. Beyond those no encoder exists for any region, and item 1 blocks the whole: an edited image needs a valid CHECK SUM block nobody here can compute, and until the routine is traced there is no knowing what the cart does with a wrong one. sram.js · tiles.js

    What closes it: item 1, then one round trip — write, boot in the emulator, see the sample play.

  9. 9

    A second fixtureNO TRACE

    Everything above rests on one dump, dezaemon-sfc-sample.sav, whose lower 64 KB equals ROM 0x50000-0x60000 byte for byte — the factory sample game, never edited — and whose upper 64 KB is zero. So the tile codec is locked by synthetic bytes only, the 18-column width has never been seen through real graphics, MY SHIP ODR and ENDING GROUP are empty, and every measured shape is one game's. The ROM is not committed and fixtures/ is gitignored, so the fixture-gated tests still report ignored; a copy of the same sample now rides along as dev-fixtures/debug-tools/Dez SNES.sav, but it is that dump byte for byte and so adds nothing to any open item. A genuine second dump has since arrived — NovaSquirrel's ALDI Adventure, published to the SFC library with 1,947 of 2,048 graphics tiles used and all 24 enemies filled — which already decided item 1's two claims; items 2, 4 and 5 are the ones that now have a diff to start from. test/_fixtures.js

    What closes it: any save written by a real session, or the freely shared “Shooting Monner” .srm; sfc:probe report says at once whether its graphics bank is present, and sfc:probe diff is built for the pair. It is the first thing to add: items 2, 4 and 5 all start from a diff against it.

02Already decoded

Confirmed The bytes decode as the ROM's label says and a test locks it — never that a runtime consumes them. Rows regions.js calls likely or open say so: the stride is what the code applies, the semantics belong to section 03 — or, for the graphics bank, to item 2.

AreaCoverage
ContainerA raw 131,072-byte dump — no header, no interleave, no compression — of the 128 KB battery SRAM that LoROM maps as four 32 KB segments in banks $70-$73. Recognised by size plus the ASCII magic T.TABATA at 0x7FF8, the programmer's initials (ROM credits TSUTOMU TABATA 94/01/27); a 65,536-byte file is accepted too, everything but GRAPIC DATA lying below 0x10000. The boot code insists on exactly 128 KB — it writes $707E7B, the MOUSE SPEED word, and reads it back through the mirror — which is why copiers with more SRAM fail its S-RAM CHECK!. isSfcSav(); splitSegments(), four views tagged with their bank, present / blank; readChecksumBlocks(). sram.js
The ROM's own mapAt ROM 0x66A5 the game keeps a debug table ADDRESS NAME, 26 rows of XXXXX-XXXXX NAME; regions.js is that table verbatim, spelling included (SCROLL EFECT, GRAPIC DATA), with a confidence and a note per row, and coverage() proves the 26 regions tile 0x00000-0x20000 with no gap or overlap. parseRomRegionTable() reads the table back and regionTableMatches() holds the two equal; readRomHeader() reads the internal header at 0x7FC0 — title DEZAEMON, map mode 0x30 (LoROM, FastROM), cart type 0x02 (ROM + SRAM + battery), size codes 0x09 / 0x07 for 512 KB / 128 KB, checksum valid — skipping a 512-byte copier header; romDefaultImage() / compareWithRomDefault() pull the 64 KB factory image at 0x50000, the one the game copies into fresh SRAM, and score a save against it. rom.js · regions.js
PalettesPALETTE DATA 0x40-0x340: 24 rows × 16 BGR555 little-endian words, R bits 0–4, G 5–9, B 10–14 — the Saturn's own layout, so rgb555ToRgb() from decode-cg.js converts unchanged and rowsToPalettes() feeds indexedToRgba(); colour 0 of a row is transparent. Rows 0–21 are colour (bit 15 clear): five identical default rows at 0x100, 0x120, 0x140, 0x160 and 0x180, a blue-grey ramp from 0x2A0, a red-to-yellow-to-white one from 0x2C0. Rows 22–23 (0x300-0x340) carry R/G/B ramps and primaries and each ends in a bit-15 word (0x87F7, 0xB76E) — the editor's own colour picker, by the look of it — so decodePaletteData() reports colorRowCount (22 in the sample) rather than assuming. cgram.js
Stage mapsLikely. MAP DATA 0x340-0x3940: 6 stages × 0x900, one byte per 16×16 chip indexing MAP GROUP, decoded as 18 columns × 128 rows (288 × 2,048 px, two chips wider than the 256-px screen). The width is measured, not assumed: rowContinuity(), the share of cells equal to the cell one row down, is highest at 18 against every non-multiple width in every sample stage (0.17–0.40) while 16 scores 0.01–0.20; its multiples 36 and 54 score as harmonics, 36 edging 18 by 0.002 in stage 4; 0x900 / 18 is exactly 128; rendered at 16 the scenery shears into diagonals, at 18 it stands upright; a fixture test asserts 18 beats 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 32 and 64 in every stage. Per stage: used, flagged, maxChip — the sample's non-zero cells run 1823 / 1794 / 1931 / 1495 / 712 / 698. map.js
Scroll tablesLikely. SCROLL EFECT 0x3940-0x4540 as 6 × 0x200 views with per-stage min / max; sfc:probe png scroll plots one as a 256-tall curve. The split is by size only (item 5). map.js
Tile groupsLikely. Every GROUP region is a table of quads — four SNES BG tilemap words (8 bytes) per 16×16 object; word = bits 0–9 tile, 10–12 palette row, 13 priority, 14 hflip, 15 vflip; 0xFFFF or 0x03FF is an empty slot — MAP GROUP 192 chips, ENEMY GROUP 24 × 4, BOSS GROUP 6 × 8, TITLE GROUP 8, ENDING GROUP 3, MY SHIP GROUP 16; the two ODR regions are the same in 8-bit, ENEMY ODR 24 × 20 and MY SHIP ODR 16. classifyQuad() names a chip [n, n+1, n+8, n+9], a strip [n, n+1, n+1|H, n|H] or empty, else null. The counts are the ROM's and a test holds them to the region boundaries; the tile numbers are VRAM numbers with no bytes behind them (item 2). tilemap.js · groups.js

03Located but not yet understood

Likely The twelve regions whose stride and shape match the ROM's label and whose semantics nobody has traced — 52,504 bytes, sliced at the right boundaries and read no further. The measurements are all of the one sample.

  • MAP DATA 0x340-0x3940 — 6 × 0x900, 18 × 128 chips by vertical continuity; bit 7 and columns 0, 8, 16 and 17 open (item 5)
  • SCROLL EFECT 0x3940-0x4540 — 6 × 0x200 by size only; a per-stage scroll table by the label, its index unknown (item 5)
  • MAP GROUP 0x4540-0x4B40 — 192 quads, free-form picks of tiles 0x2C0-0x373, 119 wholly empty
  • MY SHIP ODR 0x4B40-0x4B80 — 16 four-byte quads, zero throughout the sample
  • ENEMY GROUP 0x4B80-0x4E80 — 24 enemies × 4 quads, tiles mostly 0x360-0x3FB, a few from 0x200 and 0x2B8, some slots empty
  • BOSS GROUP 0x4E80-0x5000 — 6 bosses × 8 quads; 16 of the 48 classify as mirrored strips [n, n+1, n+1|H, n|H], the rest as neither shape
  • TITLE GROUP 0x5000-0x5040 — 8 quads, tiles 0x2A0-0x2BF, one quad three-quarters empty (0x2B8-0x2BA absent)
  • ENDING GROUP 0x5040-0x5058 — 3 quads, all empty in the sample
  • ENEMY DATA 0x8000-0x8C00 — 24 × 128 bytes, 23 in use, record 21 blank; no field named
  • APPEAR DATA 0x8C00-0xF800 — 6 × 0x1200; every stage opens with fourteen 0x00 then fourteen 0xFF, then sparse entries; no record shape named
  • ENEMY ODR 0xF800-0xFF80 — 24 × 80 bytes = 20 four-byte quads per enemy; the repeated quads look like animation frame lists
  • MY SHIP GROUP 0xFF80-0x10000 — 16 quads, 5 of them 2×2 chips [n, n+1, n+8, n+9] from tile 0x200, which suggests a sheet eight tiles wide, the rest classifying as neither shape; the numbers are VRAM tile numbers, and which SRAM bytes they load from is item 2
  • Loose ends inside confirmed regions — the four bytes after each high score (zero in the sample); palette rows 22–23 and their bit-15 tails; the bit meanings of TITLE TYPE and KEY CONFIG; the word 0x3160 at 0x3E in the first RESERVED range

04No trace at all

Absent The three regions regions.js calls open — the checksum, the sound stream, the graphics bank — and what the Saturn pipeline has that the SFC side has no code for.

  • Any write path — no SFC counterpart to bup-write.js / game-to-save.js; the tile and CGRAM encoders exist, the checksum does not (items 1, 8)
  • The game.json mapping — no SFC map-to-game.js; the parser stays behind the ./sfc subpath and nothing in the editor or the Phaser runtime reads it (item 6)
  • An SNES core in the launcher — static/emulators.json has none, so no Super Famicom cart can be played here, edited or not, and no engine trace stands behind any claim above (item 7)
  • The sound format — SOUND DATA is sliced and nothing more; no note, bar or instrument has been read out of its 11,776 bytes, and nothing is synthesised (item 3)
  • The graphics bank — no fixture carries one, the tile-number-to-offset banking is unknown, and the sample game's art has not been located in the ROM either (item 2)
  • A second fixture — a save from a real session or the shared “Shooting Monner” .srm; every measurement above is of the factory image (item 9)
