Dezaemon PSX Parity Mapデザエモン

What @shmupx/shmup-engine/psx decodes of the two PlayStation Dezaemons, and what stands between it and the parity the Saturn map reports. Two games, one memory-card file each: Dezaemon+ (Athena, 1996, SLPS-00335 — the port of the Super Famicom cart) writes its 120 KB raw, and Dezaemon Kids! (1998, SLPS-01503) packs its two sections with the Saturn sequel's own LZSS. Unlike the SFC side, both discs are here, so this is not a statistical reading: every layout below comes out of KIDS.EXE, its play overlay GAMES.bin and MAIN.EXE, and is then held against 165 community saves. What it is not is a consumer — as of 2026-09-12 the parser is a read-only structural pass: nothing is played, mapped into game.json or written back. Canonical notes: packages/shmup-engine/FORMAT-PSX.md.

01What remains

The trace landed in one pass (2026-09-11/12) once both discs were in dev-fixtures/: the containers, both directories, every region boundary, the palette bank, a probe that renders stages as the games draw them, and 29 tests over all 165 saves. What follows is what that pass could not settle. MEANING OPEN means the bytes are located at a stride the code gives but their semantics are untraced; NO TRACE means nothing exists yet. Each item ends with what would close it — and for almost all of them that is the same cheap thing, a controlled delta: change one setting in the editor under an emulator, save, and psx:probe diff the two.

  1. 1

    The Kids! option bytes, and most of its config headerMEANING OPEN

    The tail's settings are 0x1C bytes at +0xA0 — the factory initialiser stops at +0xBB and no instruction anywhere forms an address past it, so the 0x44 bytes after them are uninitialised RAM the saver copies out with the rest — and seven of them have traced readers — screen width, stereo, preset BGM, SE mute, the BGM file number and volume, and +0xB8 bit 0, which is the backdrop colour because it is index 0 of the map CLUT. The rest have structure and no names, including +0xB8 bit 1, which is set in 96 of the 98 saves and in every disc sample and has no reader anywhere. The 0x70-byte config header at data offset 0xD140 is the same story from the other end: scroll direction, the last-stage flags, ship speed and the background set are named; bytes 1, 2, 26, 51-58 and the sixteen four-byte sound-like entries at +0x23 are not. Closing it needs the editor's settings screen on screen — its labels are drawn from PELON glyph structures, not stored as Shift-JIS strings, so no amount of static reading will produce a name.

  2. 2

    The Kids! footprint marksMEANING OPEN

    105,489 bytes across the collection sit in APPEAR with the present bit clear and a class of 5, on cells that a bigger enemy covers; the play spawner skips them. Their low nibble looks exactly like a back-pointer to the owning cell — and it is not, or not in any of the six sign and axis orderings: the best, owner = (row + (n & 3), col − (n >> 2)), lands on a present byte for 60.4% of them and the misses point at other footprints. The parser carries the nibble raw as mark. The editor routine that stamps them, near 0x8008A5AC, is the place to look.

  3. 3

    The behaviour fields on both sidesMEANING OPEN

    Kids!'s five-byte enemy record is traced as far as what its bits feed — movement pattern and variant, hit points, score class, shot timing, fire interval, shot pattern — with byte 3's top bits and byte 4 unnamed, and the boss record's three four-byte sub-records untouched beyond the size class in byte 0. Dezaemon+'s 60 eight-byte definitions are sliced and their consumers listed and nothing more, and its 32-byte boss definition likewise. This is the one place the Saturn map is far ahead: decode-enemy.js and decode-boss.js name every field of a Dezaemon 2 record because the SH-2 trace went that deep.

  4. 4

    Dezaemon+'s sprite-sheet layout, settings and global configMEANING OPEN

    The 0x140 piece per stage is the chip layout of the stage's 256×320 sprite sheet, which 0x8001C82C blits to VRAM in four sub-blocks — one a plain 16-wide grid, three with interleaved addressing. The geometry is traced; not one byte's meaning is. (It was first taken for the Super Famicom's ODR by analogy, which it is not.) The eight settings bytes come from four separate variables (u8, u8, u16, u32) and none is named; the three game config bytes inside the global block are claimed to carry a pointer-table index, a level and a stage count, which is plausible and unverified. Nor is it settled which of the two 0xA0 high-score tables belongs to which game mode.

  5. 5

    Which tile buffer a save's graphics areMEANING OPEN

    A Dezaemon+ MAP GROUP word is clamped to 1023 by its reader, so it is a ten-bit tile number over the program's four 256×256 tile buffers, while a save carries 64 KB — two of them. Which two is edition-dependent and unresolved: the community saves name buffers 0 and 1, the Select 100 disc's own sample game names 2 and 3, and both render correctly only when the buffer's low bit picks the half of the save's 512-row bitmap. That rule is what the parser uses and what the renders vindicate, but the reason the two populations differ — two builds loading the graphics to different RAM — is inference, not trace.

  6. 6

    The sound streamsMEANING OPEN

    Dezaemon+'s 0x2E00 is sixteen songs of 0x2E0, and the bit layout is read off the unpacker: 16 bars of a 14-bit header and 32 steps of 11 bits, then a 16-bit tail, 734 bytes of the 736 available. What the fields mean — the bar header's 4/2/4/4 bits, the tail's 3/5/4/4, the note table at 0x8007E4EC — is not read, and nothing is synthesised. Kids! keeps no song data in the save at all: its BGM is a file number into the disc.

  7. 7

    The game.json mappingNO TRACE

    The Saturn side has map-to-game.js, and a Dezaemon 2 save becomes a playable level here. Nothing PSX exists: parsePsxSav() stays behind the ./psx subpath and nothing in the editor or the Phaser runtime reads it. The blocker is item 3 — a map is only worth writing when the records it carries are named — not the geometry, which is done for both games.

  8. 8

    Playing oneNO TRACE

    Here the PSX side starts ahead of the SFC and stops short anyway. The launcher has a PlayStation core — static/emulators.json lists mednafen psx under EmulatorJS at /psx/play.html — so unlike a Super Famicom cart, a Dezaemon disc could boot. What is missing is the wiring: no counterpart to /api/dezaemon-disc to hand the player a bring-your-own disc, and no counterpart to static/saturn-saves.js to write a memory-card image into the core's save file. Until one exists, no claim on this page has ever been checked against a running game.

  9. 9

    The write pathNO TRACE

    No PSX writer, and the two games ask for very different work. Dezaemon+ is nearly free: the layout is a table and plusChecksums() already computes what the last 0x28 bytes must be, so a writer is a scatter and a sum. Kids! needs the LZSS encoder — the Saturn's compress.js emits the same stream, so that part exists — plus the three byte sums and the sector padding, which is stale buffer content in a real save and would be zero in a written one. Neither is built.

  10. 10

    A save written by a real sessionNO TRACE

    Every measurement here is of saves the community made with the retail games, which is a far better corpus than the SFC side's single factory image — but it is still all observation. Nothing has been round-tripped: no save has been loaded into an emulator, edited by one setting, saved back and diffed. That one experiment would close most of items 1, 4 and 5 at once, and it is what psx:probe diff was built for.

02Already decoded

Confirmed The code says it and the data agrees — never that a runtime consumes the result, which nothing yet does. "All 98" and "all 67" mean the whole community collection; the discs' own sample games are counted separately where they matter.

FeatureWhat is decoded, and what pins it
ContainerA PlayStation memory card: 128 KB, 16 blocks of 8 KB, block 0 the directory — MC, then fifteen 128-byte frames of status, size, next-block link and a 20-byte file name, each ending in the XOR of its first 127 bytes. Both games write one 15-block file taking the whole card, BISLPS-00335DEZA or BISLPS-01503DEZAKIDS. locateSaves() also peels a DexDrive .gme, a single-save .mcs, a PS3 .psv and bare blocks.
Save headerThe standard SC title frame and one 16×16 4bpp icon with its own CLUT. Kids! writes the user's game name into the Shift-JIS title between 『 』 and bracketedName() reads it back; Dezaemon+'s title is fixed text assembled from five pieces of a template in the program, so its game name exists only as a logo drawn in the graphics.
Kids! directory and checksumsEleven u32 at 0x100: two compressed sizes, three offsets, three byte sums and two sector-padded lengths. Every arithmetic relation and all three checksums hold on all 98 saves. The sums run over the padded span, which matters: the padding is stale staging-buffer content and is non-zero in 96 of them.
Kids! compressionThe Saturn Dezaemon 2's Okumura LZSS, unchanged — src/decompress.js opens every section of every save, and KIDS.EXE carries both halves of the codec (encoder 0x8006C904, decoder 0x8006CD10). The two sections decompress to exactly 262,144 and 64,712 bytes, always, and land in RAM back to back, which is why the disc's thirteen SGM_* sample games are literally those two blocks concatenated.
Kids! graphics and the paletteFour 64 KB Dezaemon 2 CG pages, 256 cells of 16×16 at 8bpp. The colours are not in the save: the bank is G256PAL.CLT in the disc's KIDS_DAT.BIN, 256 RGB555 words that KIDS.EXE uploads as one 256×1 VRAM row, so a pixel byte is the index — no (row<<4)|colour indirection as on the Saturn. Embedded in kids-palette.js; the twin G256PAL2 differs in one word, index 0, which is the backdrop for the map and transparent for sprites.
Kids! stage maps6 × 0x1500: 384 rows of 7 chips, one u16 each. A chip is 32×32 px drawn from the CG cells n, n+1, n+8, n+9 with h- and v-flip bits and a blank bit. Pinned by 530,520 non-blank chips across all 98 saves in which bits 0 and 3 of the cell number are never set — the 2×2 alignment that reading requires and that a flat 16×16 reading would not produce — and by the renders, which come out as real scenery where the flat reading doubles every feature side by side.
Kids! scroll and placementSCROLL is one nibble per 64 px, 192 per stage, indexing 0, 0.25, 1 and 4 px per frame for a vertical game; APPEAR is 6 × 0xD80, 384 rows of 9 slot bytes of present | class | id. Three independent agreements: 384 map rows × 32 px, 192 scroll units × 64 px and 384 appear rows × 32 px are all 12,288 px, the wrap the renderer uses. No nibble above 3 and never two bosses in a stage, over all 588 stage blocks.
Dezaemon+ region tableThe 74-entry scatter/gather table at MAIN.EXE 0x8005A380, reproduced in plus.js; its lengths sum to exactly 0x1E000 and every region is named by the RAM array it loads into. A stage block is 0x223C, not the 0x2240 a byte-stride guess gives.
Dezaemon+ checksumsTwenty u16, one per flag group, accumulated while the file is gathered: cs[flag] += byte × (offset & 0x1F) + entryIndex, plus the entry index again at every 128-byte frame. plusChecksums() reproduces the stored 0x28 bytes of all 67 saves and of none under a 0x2240 stride — which is what makes the table self-proving rather than merely plausible, since the sum depends on each byte's offset inside its own entry. It is also the parser's integrity check: a corrupt save names the group, and the probe names the regions that group covers. One quirk: a byte at an offset that is a multiple of 32 is multiplied by zero, so one byte in 32 is invisible to it.
Dezaemon+ maps and graphics64 KB of 4bpp at a 128-byte pitch — 256 px wide, 512 rows, two texture pages — and 24 palette rows of RGB555, which the draw code picks by what it is drawing (the map takes the stage's own row, enemies 6+stage, the boss 12+stage). A map row is eight chip bytes, a byte of v-flip bits for them, then the same again: 16 chips of 16×16 px. Bit 7 of the flip byte is the leftmost chip — over 43,520 rows the bits belonging to the two columns that never carry a chip are the two that are never set, and the opposite order would put a fifth of the flips on empty chips instead of 4.7%.
Dezaemon+ stage piecesThe nine pieces of a stage block, in file order, each named by its RAM array: MAP, SCROLL, MAP GROUP, ENEMY GROUP, ENEMY DATA, SPRITE LAYOUT, APPEAR, stage config, BOSS GROUP. APPEAR is 256 records of 14 bytes and a 1,024-entry row table; its 0xFF end mark sits in record 1 of all 335 stage blocks, and of 113,756 spawn bytes not one carries a class nibble outside 8-C or D, nor an index past the 60 definitions.
Probe tooldeno task psx:probe: report (regions with a confidence, per-stage contents, checksums, high scores with the stage each run reached), png (icon, the graphics bank, the palette bank, and stage maps drawn the way each game draws them — Kids! as 32×32 chips through the disc palette, Dezaemon+ through the stage's tile table), hex (region-aware, and --section reaches inside Kids!'s decompressed sections), diff (which compares those sections, not the compressed bytes), all.
TestsFour files, 29 tests. The synthetic ones always run — a card, a Kids! block packed with the engine's own LZSS encoder, a Dezaemon+ block whose checksums are written the way the game writes them. The fixture-gated ones run the whole collection when it is present: every directory, every checksum, the chip alignment, the scroll range, one boss per stage, and every Dezaemon+ map row's empty first and last column.

03Located but not yet understood

Likely Eleven regions across the two games whose boundaries the program gives and whose contents are read only as far as a field has a name. Every one is sliced at the right offset; none is guessed at from byte statistics.

  • Kids! CONFIG 0xD140-0xD1B0 — 0x70 bytes, of which the scroll direction, the last-stage flags, ship speed and the background set are named; 0x70 is the gap to the record base rather than the used size, since nothing touches past +0x6E (item 1)
  • Kids! RECORDS 0xD1B0-0xD714 — 6 × 0xE6: 40 five-byte enemy records at class bases 0/16/24/32, then two 15-byte boss records at +0xC8 that pair with the two boss sprite tables; the fields are partly named (item 3)
  • Kids! SHIP 0xD714-0xD8C8 — 0x1B4 bytes of cell words in the map's encoding: the two ships' three poses each, their shots, the item icons and the title blocks, at offsets the VRAM uploader gives
  • Dezaemon+ SCROLL EFECT — 256 steps of 64 px naming one of the 32 four-row map blocks, then 256 effect bytes; 256 × 64 px is the same 16,384 px of stage the appear row table covers, which is the strongest thing said about it
  • Dezaemon+ APPEAR — 256 × 14-byte records plus a 0x400 row table indexed by scroll ÷ 16; the class nibble picks a size and a definition, and the arithmetic never overflows the 60 definitions in any save
  • Dezaemon+ ENEMY DATA — 60 × 8 bytes and a 32-byte boss at +0x1E0, with one stage-config byte per definition; sliced, and the fields unnamed (item 3)
  • Dezaemon+ ENEMY GROUP and BOSS GROUP — 64 and 32 u16 tile words per stage, the same word format MAP GROUP uses
  • Dezaemon+ GLOBAL — 0x164 in eight pieces carrying the Super Famicom's own names: TITLE TYPE, TITLE GROUP, ENDING GROUP, MY SHIP GROUP, MY SHIP ODR, a 16-byte stage list, three config bytes and BGM PATCH (item 4)
  • Dezaemon+ SOUND — sixteen 0x2E0 songs whose bit layout is read off the unpacker and whose fields are not (item 6)
  • Kids! and Dezaemon+ sprite and tile tables — Kids!'s 6 × 0x600 per-stage tables are confirmed to the cell, but which of an entry's 16 cells is which animation frame is read from the uploader's geometry alone
  • Loose ends inside confirmed regions — the four bytes after each Dezaemon+ high score; the Kids! high-score level byte beyond its 0..3 range and the 4 a test play writes; the fourteen distinct blank map words, of which the two fills are only the commonest

04No trace at all

Absent What the Saturn pipeline has that the PSX side has no code for, and the two regions nothing has read.

  • The game.json mapping — no PSX map-to-game.js; the parser stays behind the ./psx subpath and nothing in the editor or the runtime reads it (item 7)
  • Any write path — no counterpart to bup-write.js / game-to-save.js. Dezaemon+ is a scatter and a checksum away; Kids! also needs the LZSS encoder, which exists (item 9)
  • A disc or a card in the launcher — the PlayStation core is there, but nothing hands it a Dezaemon disc or a memory-card image, so no claim here has been checked against a running game (item 8)
  • Dezaemon+ SPRITE LAYOUT — 0x140 bytes per stage whose blit geometry is traced and whose contents are not: the sheet is 256×320 and three of its four sub-blocks are interleaved (item 4)
  • The Dezaemon+ settings bytes — eight bytes from four variables, none named (item 4)
  • A round trip — no save has been edited under an emulator and diffed, which is the experiment most of the open items are waiting on (item 10)
